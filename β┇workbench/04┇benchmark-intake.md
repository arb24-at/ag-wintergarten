# Benchmark Intake

This file records screenshot and reference-page observations for AG Wintergarten content and layout planning. These notes are not client facts and must not be copied into public page text.

Use benchmark material to understand structure, visual rhythm, image use, routing behaviour, CTA placement, and the difference between a website-like service page and a flat article. Do not use it as evidence for AG Wintergarten claims.

## Benchmark Rules

- Borrow structure, not wording.
- Borrow rhythm, not proof.
- Borrow CTA placement, not response promises.
- Borrow image integration, not case-study implication.
- Reject prices, guarantees, emergency claims, reviews, certifications, years of experience, exact locations, named products, brands, manufacturers, and sales/new-build claims unless confirmed in `α┇docs/03┇client-info.md`.

## Reference 1: Popular Options Module

Page type: navigation or routing module on a conservatory website.

Likely audience state: visitor is comparing possible conservatory topics and needs a quick path to a relevant page.

Section order and behaviour:

- heading,
- compact option links in two columns,
- secondary "view all" action,
- right-side image/promo card with a strong CTA.

Usable structure:

- concise routing module for homepage or service-cluster pages,
- option lists with short labels,
- clear route to next page.

Usable layout rhythm:

- quick scanning before deeper reading,
- links grouped by topic rather than long explanatory paragraphs.

Usable image integration:

- an image can sit beside routing options to make the module feel more like a website than a document.

Usable CTA pattern:

- one prominent visual action can sit next to lower-commitment text links.

Things to avoid:

- new-build or dream-conservatory language,
- sales-led CTA tone,
- product catalogue framing.

AG Wintergarten-compatible takeaway:

Use this pattern for a repair-first routing section, such as "Welches Problem passt zu Ihrer Anfrage?", with links only to existing public pages or approved planned links.

## Reference 2: Conservatory Roofs Service Page

Page type: product/service information page with commercial CTA and related articles.

Likely audience state: visitor is researching roof options, replacement, or product types before a sales enquiry.

Section order and behaviour:

- header and breadcrumb,
- pale hero band with image and heading,
- explanatory text,
- roof-type sections,
- image/text cards,
- large promotional CTA block,
- related article cards,
- footer.

Usable structure:

- image-supported hero,
- distinct content sections with practical subtopics,
- related-page cards near the bottom,
- strong CTA block before footer.

Usable layout rhythm:

- alternating reading and image moments,
- narrow reading width for explanations,
- bigger visual interruption before final action.

Usable image integration:

- images can break up service explanations and help readers recognise parts or structure types.

Usable CTA pattern:

- a bottom CTA block can make the next action obvious after the page has answered enough questions.

Things to avoid:

- product catalogue tone,
- replacement/new-build sales framing,
- generic related article filler,
- cookie/WhatsApp overlays as content inspiration.

AG Wintergarten-compatible takeaway:

For repair pages, use the image-led rhythm only where it supports diagnosis or enquiry preparation. Keep the content repair-first and inspection-aware.

## Reference 3: German Lamellendach Lead-Gen Page

Page type: German commercial landing page for a product-led local enquiry.

Likely audience state: visitor is comparing a substantial exterior-home product and may be ready to request a quote.

Section order and behaviour:

- split hero with large image and text,
- alternating image/content bands,
- benefit section,
- price/cost section,
- FAQ accordion,
- large dark contact form block,
- footer.

Usable structure:

- strong first viewport with image and local intent,
- alternating image/content sections,
- benefit or fit-check section,
- FAQ before contact form,
- prominent bottom form/contact area.

Usable layout rhythm:

- clear visual bands make the page feel like a business website,
- repeated image support keeps long pages from feeling like documents.

Usable image integration:

- large lifestyle/product images can establish context, but AG Wintergarten should use approved repair/service images or neutral structure images instead.

Usable CTA pattern:

- a substantial bottom contact block can work well for local service enquiries.

Things to avoid:

- prices or cost claims,
- product-brand claims,
- unsupported benefits,
- certification, award, or review-style trust language,
- exact location claims beyond confirmed broad service area.

AG Wintergarten-compatible takeaway:

Use the lead-gen structure carefully: strong local service hero, practical fit-checking sections, FAQ, and bottom enquiry block. Replace sales proof with diagnostic usefulness and clear inspection boundaries.

## Reference 4: Winter Garden Article Page

Page type: educational/blog article with sidebar and contact form.

Likely audience state: visitor is researching broad winter garden questions rather than trying to solve one repair issue.

Section order and behaviour:

- header,
- image-led hero with text overlay,
- long article body,
- sidebar with recent posts,
- many supporting images,
- contact form near bottom,
- footer.

Usable structure:

- hero image and overlay can create immediate page identity,
- images can support long explanations,
- contact can appear after educational content.

Usable layout rhythm:

- image breaks make dense information easier to scan.

Usable image integration:

- contextual images can clarify concepts, but they must be relevant and approved.

Usable CTA pattern:

- bottom contact form after education is useful, but core AG Wintergarten service pages need earlier service-enquiry cues too.

Things to avoid:

- blog-sidebar pattern for core commercial pages,
- broad article drift,
- generic educational headings unrelated to a real repair enquiry,
- B2B or buying/selling language outside the current private-first scope.

AG Wintergarten-compatible takeaway:

Borrow image pacing and readability only. Keep core pages commercial, diagnostic, and repair-first rather than article-led.

## Cross-Benchmark Conclusions

Homepage influence:

- Use a strong hero, quick fit check, routing module, common enquiry reasons, and clear contact path.
- Routing should feel scannable and website-like, not like a long paragraph of links.

Service-page influence:

- Use image-supported sections, clear fit and work-area blocks, FAQ, related links where useful, and a closing enquiry block.
- Keep each page's commercial job clear.

Problem-page influence:

- Lead with what the owner notices first, then causes, observations, inspection limits, enquiry preparation, FAQ, and CTA.
- Use images to clarify zones or symptoms, not to decorate.

Things to avoid:

- new-build positioning,
- broad product sales pages,
- article sidebars on core service pages,
- prices,
- unsupported proof,
- generic SEO sections,
- copied competitor wording,
- exact local claims that are not confirmed.
