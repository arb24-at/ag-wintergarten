# Content Intake And Page Briefs

This file collects owner/client input and page-level briefs for public website content. Keep open questions here until they are answered. Move confirmed facts to `α┇docs/03┇client-info.md` before using them as claims in public copy.

Use this file before creating or rewriting any page in `ω┇site/src/content/pages/*.md`.

Important pages should not be drafted until the brief also contains a mini evidence pack. Use `β┇workbench/04┇benchmark-intake.md` when screenshots or reference pages have been supplied.

## Owner Intake Questions

### Customer Language

- What do customers usually say when they first contact you?
- Which words do they use for leaks, seals, glass, panels, roofs, or sliding elements?
- What are the most common misunderstandings customers have?
- Which questions come up again and again before an appointment?

### Common Damage Types

- Which damage patterns do you see most often?
- Which problems are usually simple to inspect?
- Which problems often reveal hidden causes?
- Which signs suggest the issue is urgent but not an emergency-service promise?
- Which signs suggest that a repair may not be enough?

### Repair Boundaries

- Which services are definitely offered?
- Which services are only inspected or assessed?
- Which services are not offered?
- Which materials, systems, or constructions are not handled?
- Which damage types should be rejected or referred elsewhere?

### Process

- What should a customer do first?
- Which contact channel should be preferred once public details are confirmed?
- Which photos are most useful?
- What information is required before a useful first response?
- When is a `Prüfung vor Ort` needed?
- What happens after the inspection?

### Service Area And Access

- Which Berlin districts, Brandenburg towns, or nearby cities may be named publicly?
- Are there preferred or excluded areas?
- Are there access constraints that affect enquiries?
- Are roof, terrace, garden, parking, or ladder access details useful for triage?

### Proof Assets

- Which photos may be used publicly?
- Which before-and-after examples may be used publicly?
- Are there approved examples of typical damage?
- Are there approved process photos?
- Are exact certification names, partnerships, reviews, or years of experience approved?

### Benchmark And Layout References

- Which screenshots or reference pages should influence this page type?
- What section order, image rhythm, routing behaviour, or CTA placement is worth borrowing?
- What should be avoided because it is too sales-led, too generic, too article-like, or outside the confirmed repair-first scope?
- Which references are only layout inspiration and not claim sources?

## Active Page Briefs

### Page: Wintergarten undicht

URL: `/wintergarten-undicht/`

Status: active, needs SEO review and client proof inputs.

Draft file: `ω┇site/src/content/pages/wintergarten-undicht.md`

Page goal: convert water-ingress and leak enquiries without overdiagnosing.

Page job in one sentence: help a homeowner with visible water or moisture narrow what to observe and send a useful leak enquiry.

Page type: problem/service page.

Search intent: urgent problem solving and local service enquiry.

Target reader state: worried about water after rain, not yet sure whether it is roof leakage, connection leakage, drainage, seal failure, or condensation.

Primary keyword: `wintergarten undicht`

Secondary keywords: `wintergarten dach undicht`, `wintergarten abdichten`, `wasser dringt in wintergarten`

Keyword ownership: keep leak, water ingress, roof leak, and Abdichtung diagnosis intent here; keep broader repair and refurbishment intent on `/wintergarten-reparatur-berlin/`.

Homeowner scenario: water is visible inside the winter garden after rain, at the roof, near a wall connection, along a profile, or around glazing.

What the reader probably wants to know first: whether the visible water means the winter garden is leaking, what they should document, and whether a simple seal is likely enough.

What the page should help them do next: describe the leak clearly, collect safe photos, note weather behaviour, and enquire through phone, contact form, or WhatsApp.

What the page must not drift into: a general repair page, an emergency-service promise, a guaranteed diagnosis, a condensation article, or a price page.

Confirmed services this page may mention: leak inspection, cause finding, Abdichtung, Dichtungen, glass, roof and wall connections, Stegplatten, Entwässerung, doors, windows, and sliding elements on winter garden structures.

Excluded or risky claims: no fixed prices, emergency response, same-day service, guarantees, exact districts, case studies, reviews, certifications, or named products.

Likely symptoms: drips, wet profiles, damp floor, wet wall connection, water after wind-driven rain, hard or shifted seals, previous sealing that no longer works.

Likely causes: ageing seals, roof or wall connection, glazing edge, Stegplatten, profile path, drainage route, blocked outlet, condensation confusion.

What the customer should observe: when water appears, whether wind direction matters, where the first visible wet point is, whether it repeats, whether previous repairs exist.

Photos/details that help: interior wet point, overview of the winter garden, accessible exterior connection, roof edge from a safe position, profile or seal close-up, drainage point if visible.

What can be checked: roof, seals, profiles, connections, glazing, Stegplatten, drainage, and related moving elements when relevant.

What remains dependent on inspection: exact entry point, whether Abdichtung is enough, whether adjacent components need repair, and whether condensation is involved.

Benchmark influence: use the supplied references only for website rhythm: practical hero, image-supported diagnostic sections, compact FAQ, and bottom enquiry block. Do not use sales/new-build claims, pricing, product names, or blog sidebar patterns.

Mini evidence pack: observations include water after rain, damp profiles, wet floor areas, wind-driven rain, repeated wet points, old seal traces, visible seal damage, blocked drainage, and possible condensation. Affected zones include roof, wall connection, glazing edge, profiles, Stegplatten, drainage, doors, windows, and sliding elements. Useful terms include `Wassereintritt`, `Dachanschluss`, `Wandanschluss`, `Dichtung`, `Profil`, `Entwässerung`, `Abdichtung`, and `Prüfung vor Ort`.

False assumptions to avoid: the visible drip is not always the entry point, every damp mark is not automatically rainwater ingress, and new sealant is not always the correct repair.

CTA path: describe the leak, include photos if safe, mention location and weather behaviour, then use phone, contact form, or WhatsApp.

Internal links: homepage and `/wintergarten-reparatur-berlin/`.

Proof gaps: real leak photos, typical approved damage examples, process details, preferred CTA order, exact public contact details.

Owner questions: what photos are most useful for leak enquiries, how condensation should be explained from business experience, and whether any leak cases should be declined.

QA notes: current public page is cautious and service-led; future rewrite should add more real customer language once collected.
